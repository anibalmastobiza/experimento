# References and source verification

Checked on 19 October 2026. These sources motivate the research design. They do not validate the implemented task.

## 1. Pricing preferences

Schlereth, C., Skiera, B., & Schulz, F. (2018). Why do consumers prefer static instead of dynamic pricing plans? An empirical study for a better understanding of the low preferences for time-variant pricing plans. *European Journal of Operational Research, 269*(3), 1165-1179. [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejor.2018.03.033](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejor.2018.03.033).

Verification: the [publisher record and abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0377221718302613) establish authorship, publication details, methods and main claims. An [author-uploaded manuscript](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/324119900_Why_do_Consumers_prefer_Static_instead_of_Dynamic_Pricing_Plans_An_Empirical_Study_for_a_better_Understanding_of_the_Low_Preferences_for_Time-Variant_Pricing_Plans) provides the sample details: 906 questionnaires completed, 127 excluded, and 779 respondents analyzed; each made 12 choices. Its survey responses were linked to billing data. The source distinguishes static time-of-use pricing from both time-invariant pricing and dynamic pricing.

Scope: the supplied summary's approximate sample size and billing-data description were verified. The broad claim about commercially viable, customer-friendly static pricing in other domains is an extrapolation. The present task does not reproduce the paper's tariff alternatives, discrete-choice model or data linkage procedure.

## 2. Experimental workflow: Michael C. Frank and colleagues

Frank, M. C., Braginsky, M., Cachia, J., Coles, N. A., Hardwicke, T. E., Hawkins, R. D., Mathur, M. B., & Williams, R. (2025). *Experimentology: An open science approach to experimental psychology methods*. Stanford University. [https://doi.org/10.25936/3JP6-5M50](https://doi.org/10.25936/3JP6-5M50). Also published by MIT Press, ISBN 978-0-262-55256-1.

Verification: the [book's own citation and author list](https://experimentology.io/) and the [Stanford Language and Cognition Lab books page](https://langcog.stanford.edu/books). Relevant primary guidance appears in [Chapter 9, Design](https://experimentology.io/009-design.html), on randomization and counterbalancing, and [Chapter 12, Data collection](https://experimentology.io/012-collection.html), on clear online interfaces, progress, consent, debriefing, piloting and comprehension checks.

Scope: these methodological practices inform the application's organization. The adult token game and health AI comparison are new project-specific design choices. The task is not a replication, a validated Frank-laboratory instrument, or affiliated with Stanford. No specific software framework is attributed to the laboratory without verification.

## 3. Defining the trust construct

Jacovi, A., Marasović, A., Miller, T., & Goldberg, Y. (2021). Formalizing trust in artificial intelligence: Prerequisites, causes and goals of human trust in AI. *Proceedings of the 2021 ACM Conference on Fairness, Accountability, and Transparency*, 624-635. [https://doi.org/10.1145/3442188.3445923](https://doi.org/10.1145/3442188.3445923).

Verification: [author preprint and version history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.07487), which states acceptance to ACM FAccT 2021, and the [institutional publication record](https://cris.bgu.ac.il/en/publications/formalizing-trust-in-artificial-intelligence-prerequisites-causes-2/). The preprint was first submitted on 15 October 2020 and revised on 20 January 2021; the proceedings publication is cited as 2021.

Scope: this is a conceptual account of trust, vulnerability and warranted trust. It does not establish that a token stake is a validated trust score. The protocol therefore reports behavioral reliance and the separate subjective trust item, with explicit alternative explanations.

## 4. Open experimental materials

Stanford Language and Cognition Lab. (n.d.). *Materials*. [Laboratory materials page](https://langcog.stanford.edu/materials), accessed 19 October 2026.

Verification and scope: this primary laboratory page explains its practice of sharing research materials, code and data where possible and links to its [GitHub organization](https://github.com/langcog). It supports documenting and sharing this study's software and protocol. It does not imply that participant records should be made public or that this project is a Stanford study.

## Verification boundaries

No systematic review, meta-analysis, psychometric validation, power analysis, preregistration, or participant study was performed in preparing these documents. Tokens are virtual and have no cash value. No affiliation with Michael C. Frank, institutional endorsement, or clinical effectiveness is asserted. The custom single-item ratings and proposed token game have no cited prior validation.
