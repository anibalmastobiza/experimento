# Event Boundaries and Epistemic Deference to AI

## Project Overview
This project investigates how young children decide whom to trust during narrative uncertainty: a familiar caregiver or an artificial informant (AI/robot).  
The work is organized in two stages:
1. **Retrospective feasibility pilot (N=1, age 3)** to test protocol execution and identify design confounds.
2. **Prospective confirmatory study (larger N)** with preregistered hypotheses and analysis.

## Why This Project Matters
Children are increasingly exposed to AI-generated predictions in everyday contexts.  
A core developmental question is whether uncertainty moments (event boundaries) modulate selective trust and epistemic deference.

## Current Status
- Pilot materials and outputs are uploaded and documented.
- Pilot conclusions are **procedural/diagnostic only** (not population-level).
- Confirmatory scaling plan is prepared and will be preregistered separately.

## Pilot Scope and Validity
The pilot (`N=1`) is a proof-of-concept study and supports:
- operational feasibility,
- trial-level measurement capture,
- manipulation executability,
- confound discovery (e.g., fatigue/order, novelty, recency).

The pilot does **not** support:
- population effect estimation,
- confirmatory hypothesis testing,
- broad generalization claims.

## Confirmatory Plan (Next Stage)
The confirmatory study will use:
- larger sample size,
- preregistered hypotheses,
- predefined inclusion/exclusion rules,
- fixed analysis pipeline,
- stronger control of novelty and order effects.

Pilot data will remain separate and will not be pooled with confirmatory data.
